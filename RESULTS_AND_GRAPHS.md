# Results and Analysis

Every figure and number below is generated by [`src/generate_results.py`](src/generate_results.py). Regenerate with:

```bash
python src/generate_results.py
```

**Run configuration** — dataset: `sample_solar_data.csv` (8,760 hourly rows, 2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31) · sequence length 24 h · 40 epochs · device `cpu` · generated 2026-08-22 21:03 UTC.

> The bundled dataset is **synthetic** — a Spencer/Iqbal clear-sky model with stochastic monsoon-aware cloud cover, calibrated to Mumbai (lat 19.076°). It exists so the pipeline runs out of the box. Point `--data` at measured irradiance to reproduce these figures on real data.

## 2. Model Comparison

![Model Comparison](images/02_model_comparison.png)

All models share the same feature set, split, and training budget.

| Model | RMSE (W/m²) | MAE (W/m²) | R² | MAPE (%) |
|-------|-------------|------------|-----|----------|
| Persistence | 133.8 | 76.4 | 0.6401 | 76.8 |
| LSTM-only | 80.7 | 43.8 | 0.8691 | 38.7 |
| CNN-only | 80.1 | 42.1 | 0.8710 | 37.1 |
| CNN-LSTM (ours) | 80.1 | 44.9 | 0.8709 | 39.4 |

All three learned models cut RMSE by roughly **40%** against the persistence baseline, so the feature set and sequence framing are doing real work. But they land within **0.6 W/m²** of each other — a spread far smaller than the fold-to-fold variation in cross-validation below. On this dataset the CNN-LSTM hybrid shows **no measurable advantage** over either single-branch ablation; the CNN front-end and the LSTM head appear to be capturing the same structure rather than complementary structure. Treat the three as tied, not ranked.

## 7. Error Distribution

![Error Distribution](images/07_error_distribution.png)

Residuals (predicted − actual) have mean bias **-3.9 W/m²** and standard deviation **98.3 W/m²**, with excess kurtosis **4.20**. A near-zero mean indicates no systematic over- or under-prediction; the heavy tails come from rapid cloud transients.

## 8. Cross-Validation

![Cross Validation](images/08_cross_validation.png)

5-fold walk-forward validation with an expanding training window — each fold tests on the next unseen time block, never on shuffled data.

| Fold | Train samples | Test samples | RMSE (W/m²) | R² | MAPE (%) |
|------|---------------|--------------|-------------|-----|----------|
| 1 | 1,448 | 1,448 | 108.9 | 0.8371 | 37.6 |
| 2 | 2,904 | 1,452 | 107.2 | 0.8485 | 38.1 |
| 3 | 4,362 | 1,454 | 102.1 | 0.8665 | 36.3 |
| 4 | 5,820 | 1,456 | 92.8 | 0.8726 | 34.3 |
| 5 | 7,280 | 1,456 | 77.6 | 0.8753 | 36.5 |
| **Mean** | | | **97.7** | **0.8600** | **36.5** |
| Std | | | ±11.5 | ±0.0148 | ±1.3 |

Fold 1 trains on the least data and is expected to be the weakest; performance stabilises as the training window expands.

## 9. Grid Stability

![Grid Stability](images/09_grid_stability.png)

Across **365** simulated days for a 400 MW farm, with the high-ramp threshold set at 160 MW/h (40% of capacity per hour — above the median day's steepest ramp of ~32%):

- **LOW stress** (score ≥ 80): 83 days (23%)
- **MODERATE stress** (60–79): 267 days (73%)
- **HIGH stress** (< 60): 15 days (4%)

Mean stability score **74.5/100**. The score is a heuristic: it scales the day's steepest PV ramp against that threshold, so it measures ramp severity, not a physical reserve-adequacy calculation.
